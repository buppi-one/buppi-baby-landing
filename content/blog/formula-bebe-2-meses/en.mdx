Straight answer: a 2-month-old drinks a total of **700 to 950 ml (24–32 oz) of formula in 24 hours**, across **6 to 8 feeds** — which works out to **90 to 150 ml (3–5 oz) per feed depending on weight**: closer to 90–110 ml for a 4.5 kg baby, reaching 130–150 ml for bigger ones. Wide ranges on purpose: your baby can be perfectly healthy anywhere inside them.

This guide covers only month 2 — the weight-based fine-tuning, the rhythm that starts to organize itself (better nights included), the passing effect of the 2-month vaccines on appetite, and the signs the amount is right. For every age at once, see the [formula amounts by age guide](/en/blog/how-much-formula-by-age/); for the previous month, the [1-month-old guide](/en/blog/how-much-formula-1-month-old/).

## The right math is by weight, not by age

"2 months old" describes 4.5 kg babies and 6.5 kg babies — and they don't drink the same amount. The practical reference is still **about 150 ml of formula per kilogram of body weight per day** — the AAP cites ~165 ml/kg ("2.5 oz per pound") as typical, and other guidelines sit between ~120 and 180; treat 150 as a conservative starting average:

| Baby's weight | Estimated total in 24 h | In practice (6–8 feeds) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.5 kg (10 lb) | ~675 ml (23 oz) | 90–110 ml per feed |
| 5 kg (11 lb) | ~750 ml (25 oz) | 95–125 ml per feed |
| 5.5 kg (12 lb) | ~825 ml (28 oz) | 105–140 ml per feed |
| 6 kg (13 lb) | ~900 ml (30 oz) | 115–150 ml per feed |
| 6.5 kg (14 lb) | ~975 ml by the rule → in practice, the ~960 ml (32 oz) AAP ceiling | 120–150 ml per feed |

*(The 4.5–6.5 kg span covers the normal variation of the WHO growth curves at 2 months, girls and boys — extremes inside it don't mean thinness or excess; interpreting percentiles is the pediatrician's job.)*

Two honest caveats:

- The chart is a **starting point, not a target** — some days they drink more, some days less.
- Near the top of the chart sits the **~960 ml/day (32 oz) safety ceiling the AAP cites**: if your baby regularly seems to want more than that, the answer is a pediatrician conversation, not more formula.

## The typical rhythm at 2 months

Month 2 is usually when the routine **starts making sense**:

- **Intervals**: 3 to 4 hours during the day, on demand — offer at hunger cues (rooting, sucking on hands, stirring), without waiting for crying.
- **Night**: many babies start stringing together **4 to 6 hours of sleep** in one stretch. If weight gain is on track, enjoy it — no need to wake for a feed. (The caveat: babies born small for gestational age, with recent jaundice or borderline weight gain still need closer-watched feeds — in those cases, the night interval is agreed with the pediatrician.)
- **Duration**: 15 to 20 minutes is the healthy feed length — it's what the brain needs to register fullness. A 5-minute feed with an emptied bottle suggests **flow that's too fast** (switch to a slower nipple: fast flow favors overfeeding); feeds regularly beyond 30 minutes deserve a pediatrician check (inadequate flow or fatigue).
- **Paced bottle feeding**: bottle held more horizontal, pauses every 20–30 ml and side switches, letting the baby set the pace — it's the technique that best protects against bottle overfeeding.

## Growth spurts and the 2-month vaccines

Two things shake up appetite in this window — and both pass:

**Growth spurts**: the 6-week one may still be winding down, and another comes near 3 months. The pattern is similar: a few days of emptying every bottle and asking again early — duration and exact timing vary baby to baby. Before increasing, confirm it's actually hunger: at 2 months babies also suck for comfort — holding, a pacifier or a burp break resolves part of the post-bottle crying. If hunger cues persist, add 15 to 30 ml per feed, accept the higher frequency, and don't switch formulas on your own — it settles within days.

**The 2-month vaccines**: in the following 24 to 48 hours, feeding a bit less, sleeping more or being fussier is common. Offer on demand and don't force volume. A useful detail about the oral rotavirus drops: if the baby spits up right after, the dose is **not repeated** — it still counts, no readministering. Feed refusal beyond 48 hours or inconsolable crying are reasons to call the pediatrician — and fever in a young baby deserves a short ruler: low-grade fever within 48 h post-vaccine is expected, but high fever, a listless baby, or fever outside that context calls for immediate contact, no waiting. (The full schedule is in our [first-year vaccines guide](/en/blog/baby-first-year-vaccines/).)

## How to know the amount is right

The reliable indicators remain the same three:

- **Diapers**: at least **6 well-soaked a day**.
- **Weight**: average gain of **20 to 30 g per day** this trimester, assessed on the curve by the pediatrician.
- **Behavior**: a baby who feeds actively and settles between feeds is well fed.

Signs of **too much** exist too: large spit-ups after almost every feed, frequent gas and discomfort, gain far above the curve. The fix is usually slightly smaller volumes with a slow-flow nipple, respecting fullness. If it persists anyway, don't assume overfeeding: [reflux](/en/blog/baby-reflux-spit-up-when-its-gerd/) and cow's milk protein allergy (CMPA) look similar — a topic for the checkup.

> **Emergency (ER):** deep lethargy (baby hard to wake), signs of dehydration (dry mouth, sunken fontanelle, barely any wet diapers), repeated projectile vomiting. **Visit without waiting for the routine checkup:** fewer than 6 wet diapers a day, refusing several feeds beyond 48 h, weight gain falling off the curve.

## Water, tea and dilution: the rules still hold

Nothing changes here at 2 months: **no extra water and no tea** (well-prepared formula already hydrates; water in quantity can cause a dangerous sodium drop) and **dilution exactly as the label says** — in most brands, 1 level scoop per 30 ml of water (but YOUR label rules), water first. And **safe water**: boil it and mix the powder while the water is still hot, at **70 °C (158 °F) or above** — that temperature is what inactivates rare but dangerous bacteria in the powder, which is not sterile — then cool the prepared bottle to feeding temperature, testing on your wrist. Prepare fresh and follow the label. Never adjust the ratio to stretch the can or "fill them up more."

And the usual reminder for combination feeding: **breast first, top-up after**, and let diapers + the weight curve judge the whole — there is no reliable top-up chart.

## Log it and arrive sharp at the 2-month checkup

The 2-month checkup is a full one: vaccines, the curve, questions. It's far more productive with data than with memory — feeds per day, average volumes, the night sleep stretch. Logging each bottle as it happens (one tap) turns "is he eating enough?" from an impression into a number.
